Task 1 · 45 min
🔍 Secondary Image, Quote & Reference

Find a secondary image that adds something new to your research page.
Do not repeat what you already have.

⏱️ 45 minutes

Find image · 15 min → Subtitle & quote · 15 min → Reference & upload · 15 min

Write a strong subtitle

Your subtitle must say your exact research focus.

❌ Vague

  • Textures
  • Colours
  • Artist Research
  • Materials

Could be any project.

✓ Specific

  • Brutalist Concrete Surface Erosion
  • Bioluminescence in Deep Sea Organisms
  • Iris van Herpen's Kinetic Couture
  • Mycelium as Leather Alternative

Shows your focus and knowledge.

Weak vs strong
📚 Harvard referencing

Image: Artist's Last Name, First Initial. (Year). Title of the work [Image].

e.g. van Herpen, I. (2019). Hypnosis Collection [Image].

Quote: "Quote text here" (Author's Last Name, Year, p. Page Number).

Harvard Referencing Guide ↗

Steps · click to open

  • It connects to your research theme — and adds to it
  • It comes from a good source: artist website, museum, academic source
  • You can find the artist name, year and title

Ask: "If someone reads only this subtitle, do they understand my focus?"

  • Write 2–3 sentences of facts about your image or topic
  • Include at least one quote from a good source

Write a Harvard reference for your image and for your quote.

Upload your image, subtitle, facts, quote and references.

Digital skill examples

Click an image to enlarge

Steps · click to open

  • Scan at 300 DPI
  • Save as TIFF or high-quality JPEG

File › Open → your scan. Make a new layer (Layer › New › Layer…) for each:

  • Secondary image
  • Subtitle
  • Facts and quote
  • Reference

For example:

  • Threshold on your image — high contrast
  • Text on path — subtitle on a curve
  • Generative Fill — a visual background
  • Layer masks & blend modes — blend the image in
  • Custom brush — graphic marks

Stuck? Open the tutorial in the Overview tab.

Body text

12–14pt maximum

Subtitle

A different style — not just bigger

Reference

Smaller (9–10pt), near the image

  • Keep layers: File › Save As… → PSD
  • For Padlet: File › Export › Export As… → JPG
  • Take a screenshot before and after
